# Visa rules and employer targeting for an F-1 OPT new grad (as of 2026-10-20)

Researched 2026-10-20 against primary sources (Federal Register, USCIS, ICE/SEVP, eCFR, court dockets) unless marked secondary.

This is research, not legal advice. Check anything that matters with an immigration attorney or the Northeastern OGS office.

## Summary

1. The $100,000 H-1B payment (Proclamation 10973, extended to Sept 21, 2027 by Proclamation 11069) never applied to an F-1 change of status inside the US, and USCIS has not collected it since a June 8, 2026 court order that the First Circuit refused to stay on Jul 24, 2026.
2. The bigger fee risk is a DHS proposal (91 FR 54817, Aug 25, 2026) to add $103,265 to every cap-subject H-1B petition, his included. It is not final; cap-exempt employers would not pay it.
3. The weighted lottery (90 FR 60864) has been in force since FY2027 (March 2026) and has no end date. Level IV gets 4 entries, level I gets 1. DHS estimated 15.3 percent odds for level I and 30.6 for level II, against 29.6 random.
4. FY2027 had about 211,600 registrations (secondary), down from 343,981 in FY2026 (120,141 selected). Level I was 17.8 percent of selections and levels III and IV were 46.3 percent (Proclamation 11069).
5. Cap-gap now runs to April 1. If he is on STEM OPT, a March 2027 selection is easy: he keeps working until an Oct 1, 2027 H-1B start.
6. STEM OPT needs an E-Verify employer, a signed I-983, commensurate pay and a direct employer relationship. Startups qualify; staffing and labor-for-hire firms do not.
7. OPT is under threat. A proposed rule (91 FR 64566, comments due Nov 9, 2026) would make schools pay $70,000 or $30,000 before recommending OPT, which could hit his April 2027 STEM OPT. The duration of status rule is enjoined.
8. Cap-exempt employers (universities, affiliated nonprofits, research nonprofits, government research) skip the lottery and the proposed fee and pay about $960 in government fees. A small cap-subject company pays about $2,225 today.
9. For targeting, rank E-Verify enrollment first (hard gate by July 2027), then cap-exempt status, then pay at level II or above, then direct-employer status and past H-1B filings.
10. With STEM OPT he gets three lotteries (March 2027, 2028, 2029). By my estimate that is about 67 to 92 percent cumulative odds at level II and 39 to 72 percent at level I. Without STEM OPT he gets one.

## 1. The $100,000 H-1B payment (Proclamations 10973 and 11069)

Note on numbering: the September 2025 proclamation is **Proclamation 10973**. **Proclamation 11069** is the September 2026 extension. Both are titled "Restriction on Entry of Certain Nonimmigrant Workers".

### 1.1 What the proclamations say

- Proclamation 10973 was signed Sept 19, 2025 and published at 90 FR 46027 (Sept 24, 2025). It restricts entry of H-1B workers "except for those aliens whose petitions are accompanied or supplemented by a payment of $100,000." It took effect 12:01 a.m. EDT Sept 21, 2025 and was set to expire 12 months later "absent extension." ([govinfo text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-24/html/2025-18601.htm), read 2026-10-20)
- Section 1(b) tells DHS to restrict decisions on petitions without the payment for H-1B workers "who are currently outside the United States." ([same](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-24/html/2025-18601.htm), read 2026-10-20)
- The only exception in the text is a national interest exception, granted by the DHS Secretary for an individual, a company, or an industry. There is no carve-out for cap-exempt employers, universities, or nonprofits in the text. ([same](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-24/html/2025-18601.htm), read 2026-10-20)
- Section 4 told DOL to start a rule to raise prevailing wage levels and told DHS to start a rule to "prioritize the admission ... of high-skilled and high-paid aliens." That second order became the weighted lottery (section 2 below). ([same](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-09-24/html/2025-18601.htm), read 2026-10-20)
- **Proclamation 11069** was signed Sept 18, 2026 and published at 91 FR 60497 (Sept 23, 2026). It extends the same restriction for 12 months: effective 12:01 a.m. EDT Sept 21, 2026, expiring "absent extension" 12 months later (to Sept 21, 2027). The operative text is the same as 10973: $100,000 payment, workers "currently outside the United States", national interest exception only. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/23/2026-19554/restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers); [full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/23/2026-19554.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- Proclamation 11069 also says: the payment "has been made for over 700 petitions"; the largest IT staffing and outsourcing firms cut combined registrations "from 24,946 to 2,055, a 92 percent decrease"; consular processing requests fell "nearly 97 percent" from FY2025 to FY2027; and the weighted selection rule "was in effect for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2027 H-1B cap season." ([full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/23/2026-19554.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- Same day, Executive Order 14431 (Sept 18, 2026) tells State, DOL and DHS to "take into account ... whether the employer sponsor directly or indirectly engaged in layoffs within the previous year or plans future layoffs that negatively affect the employment of similarly situated United States workers," and tells DOL's Wage and Hour Division to start reviewing past LCA data within 30 days. ([whitehouse.gov](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/09/enhancing-program-integrity-and-integrity-and-interagency-coordination-in-the-administration-of-the-h-1b-nonimmigrant-visa-program/), read 2026-10-20)

### 1.2 USCIS guidance: who pays, who does not

- USCIS memo of Sept 20, 2025 (Director Edlow): the proclamation "only applies prospectively to petitions that have not yet been filed" and not to current approved petitions or valid H-1B visas. ([USCIS memo PDF](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/memos/H1B_Proc_Memo_FINAL.pdf), read 2026-10-20)
- USCIS H-1B page (guidance first issued Oct 20, 2025): the payment applies to new petitions "on behalf of beneficiaries who are outside the United States and do not have a valid H-1B visa," and to petitions that request consular notification, port of entry notification, or pre-flight inspection. ([USCIS H-1B Specialty Occupations](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- **Change of status inside the US is exempt.** Same page: "The Proclamation also does not apply to a petition ... that is requesting an amendment, change of status, or extension of stay for an alien inside the United States where the alien is granted such amendment, change, or extension." A beneficiary of such a petition is not subject to the payment if they later travel and get a visa stamp on the approved petition. ([same](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- **The catch.** If USCIS finds the person ineligible for change of status, "for example, is not in a valid nonimmigrant visa status or if the alien departs the United States prior to adjudication of a change of status request," the payment applies. For an F-1 OPT worker this means: stay in valid status and do not leave the US while the change of status is pending. ([same](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- National interest exceptions are for "the extraordinarily rare circumstance" and are requested by email to H1BExceptions@hq.dhs.gov. ([same](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- Cap-exempt employers are not exempt from the proclamation payment. Neither the proclamation text nor the USCIS page lists them as exempt; what saves most cap-exempt hires of F-1 students is the same change-of-status rule. (Inference from the texts above; one secondary source, a grant-writing blog, makes the same point about 11069: [grantedai.com](https://grantedai.com/blog/h-1b-100000-payment-proclamation-extended-september-18-2026-through-september-21-2027-first-circuit-block-cap-exempt-universities-not-carved-out-consular-petitions-strategy), read 2026-10-20)

### 1.3 Court rulings

- **D. Mass., State of California v. Mullin, No. 1:25-cv-13829** (filed Dec 12, 2025 as California v. Noem). On June 8, 2026 the court "vacated agency guidance implementing the $100,000 payment requirement." The order was administratively stayed while the government sought a stay. ([USCIS alert](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/presidential-proclamation-on-restriction-on-entry-of-certain-nonimmigrant-workers), last updated 06/15/2026, read 2026-10-20; [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72031571/state-of-california-v-noem/), read 2026-10-20)
- **First Circuit, No. 26-1699.** On July 24, 2026 the First Circuit denied the government's motion for a stay pending appeal. USCIS: "DHS ... will comply with the court's order while DHS considers next steps. If this order is later lifted, DHS still plans to collect the payment." The merits appeal is still pending. ([USCIS H-1B FAQ](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/h-1b-faq), last updated 07/28/2026, read 2026-10-20; [CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73476893/state-of-california-v-mullin/), read 2026-10-20)
- The district court's reasoning (an unauthorized tax and an APA violation, summary judgment for 20 states) is from secondary sources only. ([CUPA-HR](https://www.cupahr.org/resource/federal-court-vacates-h-1b-visa-fee-policy/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/work-visas/1801034/alert-federal-court-declares-$100000-h-1b-fee-an-unlawful-tax-and-vacates-the-dhs-policy), read 2026-10-20)
- **D.D.C., Chamber of Commerce v. DHS, No. 1:25-cv-03675.** Judge Howell upheld the proclamation on Dec 23, 2025 (secondary: [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-district-court-judge-issues-opinion-upholding-dollar100000-h-1b-fee.html), read 2026-10-20). Appeal is D.C. Cir. No. 25-5473, opened Dec 31, 2025, still open on CourtListener with no termination date ([CourtListener search](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72095497/chamber-of-commerce-of-the-united-states-of-ameri-v-dhs/), read 2026-10-20). Argument was March 9, 2026 per a secondary source. I found no D.C. Circuit decision.
- Global Nurse Force v. Trump, N.D. Cal. No. 4:25-cv-08454, is another open challenge ([CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/71541425/global-nurse-force-v-trump/), read 2026-10-20). Its status was not checked.

### 1.4 Status today (Oct 20, 2026)

- USCIS is **not collecting** the $100,000 payment, because of the D. Mass. vacatur. ([USCIS H-1B page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- Proclamation 11069 extends the restriction to Sept 21, 2027. Whether the June 8 vacatur also blocks collection under 11069 is not settled on any USCIS page I found. A law firm says USCIS "should be barred" under the extension too (secondary: [Fragomen, Sept 18, 2026](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-president-trump-extends-dollar100000-h-1b-fee-but-policy-currently-vacated-by-court-order.html), read 2026-10-20).
- **New and bigger: a proposed $103,265 fee on every cap-subject H-1B petition.** DHS proposed rule "Fee for Certain H-1B Petitions," 91 FR 54817 (Aug 25, 2026), RIN 1615-AD20, comments closed Sept 24, 2026. It would add 8 CFR 106.2(a)(3)(xii): "The following additional fee is required for all H-1B cap-subject petitions, including those eligible for the advanced degree exemption ...: $103,265." It is based on fee authority (INA 286(m)), not the proclamation, and would be paid "in addition to" any proclamation payment. Cap-exempt petitions would not pay it. The text has no exemption for change of status, so an F-1 OPT worker selected in the lottery **would be covered** if it is finalized as proposed. DHS estimates it would have a significant economic impact on 11,051 small entities (76 percent of small entities that filed cap-subject petitions in FY2025). ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/25/2026-17324/fee-for-certain-h-1b-petitions); [full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/08/25/2026-17324.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- As of Oct 20, 2026 the Federal Register shows only the proposed rule and a correction (Sept 10, 2026), no final rule. ([FR API search for RIN 1615-AD20](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions%5Bterm%5D=%221615-AD20%22), read 2026-10-20)

**What it means for Devanshu.** Today, an employer filing a cap-subject H-1B for him as a change of status from F-1 inside the US owes no $100,000. That was true even before the court order. The live risk is the proposed $103,265 fee, which would hit every cap-subject petition, including his, if finalized before his employer files. Cap-exempt employers would not pay it.

## 2. The weighted (wage-level) H-1B lottery

### 2.1 Rule history

- Proposed rule: "Weighted Selection Process for Registrants and Petitioners Seeking To File Cap-Subject H-1B Petitions," 90 FR 45986, Sept 24, 2025. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/24/2025-18473/weighted-selection-process-for-registrants-and-petitioners-seeking-to-file-cap-subject-h-1b-petitions), read 2026-10-20)
- Final rule: same title, 90 FR 60864, Dec 29, 2025, effective Feb 27, 2026, "effective in time for the FY 2027 registration season." DHS finalized it "as proposed in the NPRM, without modification." ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/29/2025-23853/weighted-selection-process-for-registrants-and-petitioners-seeking-to-file-cap-subject-h-1b); [full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- It has no end date. It changes 8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(A)(4) and so applies to every future cap season until changed. ([full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- It was used for FY2027 (registration March 4 to 19, 2026). Proclamation 11069 says it "was in effect for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2027 H-1B cap season." ([USCIS registration page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations-and-fashion-models/h-1b-electronic-registration-process); [91 FR 60497](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/23/2026-19554.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- So it will apply to FY2028 (March 2027) and later unless DHS changes it or a court stops it.

### 2.2 How it works

- The registrant states "the highest Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) wage level that the beneficiary's proffered wage equals or exceeds for the relevant Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code in the area(s) of intended employment." ([USCIS registration page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations-and-fashion-models/h-1b-electronic-registration-process), read 2026-10-20)
- Each unique beneficiary enters the pool once per weight: level IV four times, III three, II two, I once. Once picked, the rest of that person's entries are removed. ([final rule text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20; also summarized in docs/research/2026-10-17-growth-signals.md in this repo)
- Wages below level I (non-OEWS sources) are grouped with level I. Several worksites use the lowest level. If several employers register the same person at different levels, the person gets the lowest level. ([final rule text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- The petition must later include "evidence of the basis of the wage level selected on the registration." ([USCIS FY2027 selection alert, 03/31/2026](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/fy-2027-h-1b-initial-registration-selection-process-completed), read 2026-10-20)
- The registration fee for FY2027 was $215 per beneficiary, non-refundable. ([USCIS registration page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations-and-fashion-models/h-1b-electronic-registration-process), read 2026-10-20)

### 2.3 Odds by wage level

**DHS's own estimate** (final rule, Table 13, a pool of 320,711 unique beneficiaries and 94,900 selections): random lottery 29.59 percent for everyone; weighted lottery 15.29 percent for level I, 30.58 for II, 45.87 for III, 61.16 for IV. That is minus 48 percent for level I, plus 3 percent for II, plus 55 for III, plus 107 for IV. DHS's own simulation, which removes a person after the first pick, gives level I 15,330 of 89,911 selected, which is 17.0 percent. ([final rule text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20)

**Plain reading:** under the weighted lottery a level I registrant has about half the odds he had under the random lottery. A level II registrant has about the same odds as before. Level II is twice as likely as level I to be picked in any one draw.

**What FY2027 actually looked like** (the first weighted lottery):
- 211,600 properly submitted registrations, down 38.5 percent from 343,981. 71.5 percent of selected beneficiaries held US advanced degrees (57 percent in FY2026). 17.7 percent of selected registrations were level I. Source: a USCIS post on X in May 2026, reported by Fragomen. **Secondary only**; I could not open the USCIS post. ([Fragomen, Jul 17, 2026](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-h-1b-cap-is-reached-for-fy-2027.html), read 2026-10-20)
- A primary source backs the wage mix: Proclamation 11069 says level III and IV "accounted for approximately 46.3 percent of H-1B registration selections while those corresponding with the lowest wage level accounted for only 17.8 percent," and registrants with at least a US master's rose "from 45.1 percent ... for FY 2026 to 66.1 percent for FY 2027." That leaves about 35.9 percent of selections at level II. ([91 FR 60497](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/23/2026-19554.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- USCIS reached the FY2027 cap on Jul 17, 2026, with no second selection round announced. ([USCIS alert](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/alerts/uscis-reaches-fiscal-year-2027-h-1b-cap), read 2026-10-20)
- USCIS has not published the FY2027 count of selected registrations or the count of registrants by wage level, so an exact FY2027 selection rate cannot be computed from primary data. (Checked the [registration page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations-and-fashion-models/h-1b-electronic-registration-process), read 2026-10-20)

**My estimate for FY2027 (labeled as an estimate, not a USCIS number).** Model: weighted picking without replacement, where a person with weight w is picked with probability 1 - t^w for one shared t. This model reproduces DHS's simulated 17.0 percent for level I. Inputs: 211,600 registrants; the selection shares above (17.8, 35.9, 46.3 percent); the DHS level III to IV mix; and an assumed number of selections, because USCIS has not published it.

| Assumed selections | Random lottery (everyone) | Level I | Level II | Level III | Level IV |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 95,000 | 44.9% | 25.9% | 45.1% | 59.3% | 69.8% |
| 105,000 | 49.6% | 29.2% | 49.9% | 64.5% | 74.9% |
| 120,141 (FY2026's count) | 56.8% | 34.6% | 57.2% | 72.0% | 81.7% |

The pattern holds in every row: level I gets about 0.6 times the random-lottery odds, level II about the same as random, and levels III and IV well above. The level counts these inputs imply are about 62,000 to 65,000 at level I, 75,000 at level II, and 71,000 to 74,000 at levels III and IV combined. A US master's holder (Devanshu) also gets a second chance in the 20,000 advanced-degree round, so his odds are somewhat higher than the averages shown. I did not model that round. With 66.1 percent of FY2027 registrants holding a US master's, the second round helps less than it used to.

### 2.4 Litigation

- I found no court order against the weighted selection rule. A Forbes column (secondary) reported on Mar 3, 2026 that no business group had sued to stop it before the FY2027 lottery. ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/stuartanderson/2026/03/03/no-business-immigration-lawsuits-filed-against-dhs-h-1b-lottery-rule/), read 2026-10-20)

### 2.5 A related rule: DOL's proposal to raise prevailing wages

- DOL proposed "Improving Wage Protections for the Temporary and Permanent Employment of Certain Foreign Nationals in the United States," 91 FR 15454, Mar 27, 2026 (RIN 1205-AC30). It would change how the four OEWS wage levels are computed for LCAs and PERM. Comments closed May 26, 2026. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/27/2026-06017/improving-wage-protections-for-the-temporary-and-permanent-employment-of-certain-foreign-nationals-in-the-united-states), read 2026-10-20)
- As of Oct 20, 2026 the Federal Register shows no final rule. Proclamation 11069 calls it a rule that "once finalized, will better align prevailing wage levels with wages paid to similarly employed and located American workers." ([91 FR 60497](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/23/2026-19554.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- If it is finalized, the salary needed to reach each level goes up. A salary that is level II today could be level I after that. The level cutoffs in growth-signals.md (OFLC 2026-27 wage year) would need to be redone. I did not extract the new proposed level formulas.

## 3. Registration counts, selection rates, and cap-gap

### 3.1 Counts from USCIS

| Cap FY | Eligible registrations | Unique beneficiaries | Selected | Selected / unique beneficiaries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2025 | 470,342 | about 442,000 | 135,137 | about 30.6% |
| FY2026 | 343,981 | about 339,000 | 120,141 | about 35.4% |
| FY2027 | 211,600 (properly submitted, secondary) | not published | not published | not published |

Sources: FY2025 and FY2026 from the [USCIS H-1B Electronic Registration Process page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations-and-fashion-models/h-1b-electronic-registration-process) (read 2026-10-20). The last column is my arithmetic. FY2026 had about 57,600 unique employers and 1.01 registrations per beneficiary (same page). FY2027 is from the secondary source in 2.3.

### 3.2 Cap-gap today

- The January 2025 H-1B modernization rule (89 FR 103054, Dec 18, 2024, effective Jan 17, 2025) moved the cap-gap end date. USCIS now says the cap-gap period "ends on April 1 of the fiscal year for which the H-1B status is being requested or until the validity start date of the approved petition, whichever is earlier." (The old end date was Oct 1.) ([USCIS cap-gap page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/extension-of-post-completion-optional-practical-training-opt-and-f-1-status-for-eligible-students), read 2026-10-20)
- Who qualifies: a cap-subject petition, based on a selected registration, "properly and timely filed," requesting change of status (not consular processing), filed while the student's F-1 status was still valid, including OPT and the grace period. ([same](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/extension-of-post-completion-optional-practical-training-opt-and-f-1-status-for-eligible-students), read 2026-10-20)
- Work: if the petition is filed while the student is working on OPT, both status and work authorization are extended. If it is filed during the grace period, status is extended but not work. ([same](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/extension-of-post-completion-optional-practical-training-opt-and-f-1-status-for-eligible-students), read 2026-10-20)
- The extension ends if the petition is denied, withdrawn, revoked, or rejected, or if change of status is denied. Cap-gap does not apply to cap-exempt petitions. ([same](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/extension-of-post-completion-optional-practical-training-opt-and-f-1-status-for-eligible-students), read 2026-10-20)
- The 2026 duration of status rule (section 4.5) says it "does not make any changes to the H-1B cap gap rule," and that rule is enjoined anyway. ([91 FR 44976 text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/07/17/2026-14439.txt), read 2026-10-20)

### 3.3 What this means if Devanshu is selected in March 2027 (FY2028)

- The employer can file from April 1, 2027 for an Oct 1, 2027 start. His OPT EAD runs to Jul 8, 2027, so a filing in April to June 2027 is made while he is on valid OPT.
- **If he moves to STEM OPT** (filed before Jul 8, 2027), cap-gap barely matters. He keeps working on STEM OPT until H-1B starts on Oct 1, 2027.
- **If he has no STEM OPT** (for example, his employer is not in E-Verify), cap-gap carries his status and work authorization from Jul 8, 2027 to Oct 1, 2027. This only works if the petition is filed and requests change of status before Jul 8, 2027.
- Either way he should not leave the US while change of status is pending. Leaving ends change of status, and under current USCIS guidance it would also trigger the $100,000 proclamation payment if collection resumes (section 1.2).
- After H-1B starts, DHS has proposed removing the up-to-60-day grace period that H-1B and other workers get after a job ends. Proposed rule "Eliminating the Discretionary 60-Day Grace Period," 91 FR 57807, Sept 11, 2026, comments due Nov 10, 2026. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/11/2026-18631/eliminating-the-discretionary-60-day-grace-period), read 2026-10-20)

## 4. OPT and STEM OPT

### 4.1 Regular post-completion OPT (what he has now)

- The work must be "directly related to the student's major area of study." ([8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(A), eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20)
- The rules put nothing on the employer here: no E-Verify, no training plan, no petition, no government fee. The employer completes a normal Form I-9 with his EAD, as for any hire. (Inference: the employer duties in 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(C) apply only to the STEM extension; [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20)
- Unemployment: "Students may not accrue an aggregate of more than 90 days of unemployment during any post-completion OPT period." ([8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20)

### 4.2 STEM OPT (24 months, what he needs by Jul 8, 2027)

Employer requirements, all from 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(C) ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20):
- **E-Verify.** "The student's employer is enrolled in E-Verify ... and the employer remains a participant in good standing." It also needs an EIN.
- **Form I-983 training plan**, signed by someone with signing authority. It must set training goals, how they will be met, an evaluation process, and supervision. "Employers may rely on their otherwise existing training programs or policies" for the evaluation and supervision parts.
- **Pay and hours** "commensurate" with similarly situated US workers, at least 20 hours a week. This applies even if the employer has two or fewer similar US workers.
- **Attestations:** enough resources to train; the student "will not replace" a US worker.
- **Reporting:** tell the school within five business days if the student leaves or is terminated. A new I-983 is needed for material changes.
- **Evaluations:** at 12 months and at the end, signed by student and employer.
- **Site visits:** DHS may visit, normally with 48 hours' notice.

Employer-employee relationship, staffing, third-party placement, startups:
- The 2016 STEM OPT rule (81 FR 13040, Mar 11, 2016) says the extension does not suit "multiple employer arrangements, sole proprietorships, employment through 'temp' agencies, employment through consulting firm arrangements that provide labor for hire, and other relationships that do not constitute a bona fide employer-employee relationship." Students must be "bona fide employees of the employer signing the Training Plan," and that employer "must be the same entity that employs the student and provides the practical training experience." ([81 FR 13040 text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2016/03/11/2016-04828.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- Startups are allowed "so long as all regulatory requirements are met," including E-Verify, the training plan, commensurate pay, and resources. Equity such as stock options can count toward pay if total pay is commensurate. ([same](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2016/03/11/2016-04828.txt); [SEVP STEM OPT Extension Overview](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/stem-opt-extension-overview), last updated Apr 28, 2025, read 2026-10-20)
- SEVP's FAQ says the only thing an employer must do to be eligible is to be "enrolled in and remain in good standing with the E-Verify" program. It also notes that if the student is exempt from FICA, the employer saves 6.2 percent of salary up to the wage base plus 1.45 percent. ([SEVP STEM OPT FAQ](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/stem-opt-frequently-asked-questions), last updated Apr 28, 2025, read 2026-10-20)

Timing and unemployment:
- The STEM I-765 can be filed "up to 90 days prior to the expiration date of the student's current OPT employment authorization," and within 60 days of the school's recommendation. If filed on time, the OPT EAD is extended up to 180 days while the case is pending. ([8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(C), eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2); [8 CFR 274a.12(b)(6)(iv)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-274a/section-274a.12), read 2026-10-20)
- For Devanshu (EAD ends Jul 8, 2027) the filing window opens about Apr 9, 2027. He needs a signed I-983 from an E-Verify employer before then.
- The October 2026 final rule that ended automatic EAD extensions for renewals (91 FR 65306, Oct 14, 2026) does not mention STEM OPT or 274a.12(b)(6)(iv), so the 180-day STEM extension appears unaffected. That is my reading of the text; no agency page confirms it. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20955/removal-of-automatic-extensions-for-certain-employment-authorization-documents), read 2026-10-20)
- Unemployment on STEM OPT: no more than 150 days in total across the whole OPT period, which means 60 more days on top of the 90. ([8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2); [SEVP FAQ](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/stem-opt-hub/stem-opt-frequently-asked-questions), read 2026-10-20)

### 4.3 Proposals to change or end OPT (status Oct 20, 2026)

1. **"Optional Practical Training Fees," proposed rule, 91 FR 64566, Oct 8, 2026** (RIN 1653-AB01, docket ICEB-2026-0100). Comments are due **Nov 9, 2026**. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/08/2026-20660/optional-practical-training-fees); [full text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/08/2026-20660.txt), read 2026-10-20)
   - The school, not the student or employer, would pay **$70,000** "the first time that the alien is recommended to participate in OPT" and **$30,000** for any later OPT recommendation if the $70,000 was paid. Payment comes before the school's recommendation, and USCIS would not grant the EAD until the fee is paid.
   - DHS says the rule "does not preclude institutions from funding the OPT fees through financial contributions from external parties, such as employers."
   - It would take effect 60 days after a final rule. Students already on approved OPT, or recommended before that date, are not charged for that OPT. But "if the F-1 nonimmigrant student submits a subsequent request for OPT on or after the effective date of the final rule, the fee provisions ... will apply."
   - **Effect on Devanshu.** His STEM OPT recommendation (around April 2027) counts as a "subsequent" OPT request. If the rule is final and in effect by then, Northeastern would have to pay before recommending him. The text does not say clearly which amount applies to someone whose first OPT predates the rule: the $30,000 tier requires that "the initial OPT fee of $70,000" was paid, which he never paid. That reading points to $70,000.
   - DHS says that without these fees "it cannot operate OPT consistent with its focus on preventing fraud and may shut down the program entirely."
2. **"Practical Training," RIN 1653-AA97** (ICE), on the regulatory agenda at the proposed rule stage with an NPRM target of February 2027. It would "address fraud and national security concerns, protect U.S. workers from being displaced by foreign nationals, and enhance the Student and Exchange Visitor Program's capacity to oversee the program." No text has been published. ([reginfo.gov](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510&RIN=1653-AA97), read 2026-10-20)
3. **Duration of status final rule**, "Establishing a Fixed Time Period of Admission ...," 91 FR 44976, Jul 17, 2026, set to take effect Sept 15, 2026. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant), read 2026-10-20) What it would do for him:
   - The post-study departure period becomes 30 days instead of 60 for new admissions.
   - New OPT or STEM OPT applications need an extension of stay (Form I-539) as well as the I-765. Students who file their STEM I-765 on or before **Mar 18, 2027** are excused from the I-539. His window opens about Apr 9, 2027, so as written he would need to file the I-539 too, unless DHS extends the date.
   - It "does not make any changes to the H-1B cap gap rule."
   - **Status: blocked.** In Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration v. DHS, D. Mass. No. 1:26-cv-13799 (filed Aug 17, 2026), Judge Saylor granted a preliminary injunction on Sept 14, 2026 and stayed the rule while the case goes on. ([CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74661796/presidents-alliance-on-higher-education-and-immigration-v-united-states/); [AILA copy of the order](https://www.aila.org/library/memorandum-and-order-granting-preliminary-injunction-on-the-elimination-of-duration-of-status-final-rule), read 2026-10-20) The scope of the injunction (nationwide, whole rule) comes from a secondary source ([Ogletree / globalimmigrationblog, Sept 16, 2026](https://www.globalimmigrationblog.com/2026/09/federal-court-blocks-dhs-f-1-j-1-fixed-period-admission-rule-curricular-practical-training-restrictions-remain/), read 2026-10-20). Note: eCFR already shows the rule's text as current ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20), so read eCFR with care.
4. **Grace period for H-1B and other workers:** the proposed removal of the 60-day grace period (91 FR 57807, Sept 11, 2026) covers workers in classes like H-1B, not F-1 OPT (section 3.3).

**Bottom line.** STEM OPT itself (E-Verify plus I-983) is unchanged in the regulations today. The serious threat is the proposed OPT fee. If it is final before April 2027, his school would have to pay (or find someone to pay) $30,000 to $70,000 before recommending his STEM extension.

## 5. Cap-exempt H-1B employers

### 5.1 Who qualifies

The statute is INA 214(g)(5)(A) and (B). The definitions are at 8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(F) and (h)(19)(iii) ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20):
- **Institutions of higher education**, as defined in the Higher Education Act, 20 U.S.C. 1001(a).
- **Nonprofits related to or affiliated with one.** Any one of these is enough: shared ownership or control by the same board; operated by the institution; attached as a member, branch, cooperative or subsidiary; or a formal written affiliation agreement for research or education, where "a fundamental activity of the nonprofit entity is to directly contribute to the research or education mission" of the institution.
- **Nonprofit research organizations and governmental research organizations.** "When a fundamental activity of a nonprofit organization is engaging in basic research and/or applied research, that organization is a nonprofit research organization." A governmental research organization can be federal, state, or local. Applied research includes work "that has specific commercial objectives with respect to products, processes, or services." Nonprofit means IRS 501(c)(3), (c)(4) or (c)(6).
- **People not employed directly by such an organization.** The beneficiary qualifies if he "will spend at least half of their work time performing job duties at a qualifying institution, organization, or entity" and those duties "directly further an activity that supports or advances" its higher education, nonprofit research, or government research purpose. Telework counts: "USCIS will focus on the job duties to be performed, rather than where the duties are physically performed."

### 5.2 What the 2024 modernization rule changed

- The rule is 89 FR 103054, Dec 18, 2024, effective Jan 17, 2025. It replaced "primarily engaged" and "primary mission" with "fundamental activity." This lets organizations that do research as one fundamental activity, "but are not primarily engaged in research," qualify. It also wrote the "at least half of their time" rule for workers not employed directly. ([89 FR 103054 text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2024/12/18/2024-29354.txt), read 2026-10-20)

### 5.3 What cap-exempt status gets the employer

- **No lottery.** "H-1B workers who are petitioned for or employed at an institution of higher education or its affiliated or related nonprofit entities, a nonprofit research organization, or a government research organization, are not subject to this numerical cap." The employer can file in any month, with no March registration. ([USCIS H-1B page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- **No ACWIA fee** for higher education, affiliated nonprofits, and nonprofit or governmental research organizations. ([8 CFR 214.2(h)(19)(iii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20)
- **No proposed $103,265 fee.** It would apply only to cap-subject petitions: "the additional fee would not apply to H-1B petitions that are not subject to the cap, such as petitions filed by certain nonprofit research organizations, governmental research organizations, and institutions of higher education." ([USCIS news release, Aug 24, 2026](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom/news-releases/dhs-proposes-additional-h-1b-fee), read 2026-10-20)
- **The $100,000 proclamation payment: no special exemption.** As covered in 1.2, cap-exempt employers are not carved out. They avoid it the same way cap-subject employers do, by filing a change of status for someone already in the US (and, today, because collection is vacated).
- **Cap-gap does not apply** to cap-exempt petitions ([USCIS cap-gap page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/extension-of-post-completion-optional-practical-training-opt-and-f-1-status-for-eligible-students), read 2026-10-20). It is not needed, though, because a cap-exempt petition can be filed with a start date at any time.
- **Concurrent employment.** A person in cap-exempt H-1B can also work a cap-subject H-1B job at the same time without being counted against the cap, as long as the cap-exempt job continues. If the cap-exempt job ends, he becomes cap-subject unless already counted. ([8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(F)(5)-(6)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2); [USCIS H-1B page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations), read 2026-10-20)
- **Scale.** DHS reports FY2025 cap-exempt H-1B approvals of 24,835 at institutions of higher education, 19,866 at affiliated or related nonprofits, 5,654 at nonprofit or governmental research organizations, and 3,634 for beneficiaries placed at a qualifying entity. That is 25,452 new employment approvals in total. ([90 FR 60864 text, footnote 39](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20)

### 5.4 Software jobs that exist there (kinds, not a verified list)

These categories follow from the definitions above. Whether a given employer qualifies has to be checked case by case:
- Universities: research software engineers in labs and research computing groups, central IT and data engineering, ed-tech and digital learning teams.
- Affiliated nonprofits: academic medical centers and teaching hospitals tied to a university (clinical data, imaging, health informatics, EHR integration), and university-affiliated research institutes.
- Nonprofit research organizations (501(c)(3) institutes with research as a fundamental activity): bioinformatics, ML research engineering, scientific computing, open-source research tools.
- Governmental research organizations: state or local government research bodies. Federal labs vary. Many national labs are run by contractors, and whether a given contractor-run lab is cap-exempt is **not verified** here.
- Private companies whose employee works at least half time on duties that further a qualifying organization's research or education mission, for example a vendor team embedded in a university research project (8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(F)(4)).

## 6. Other routes for a new grad

### 6.1 O-1A

- There is no lottery and no cap, and the $100,000 proclamation covers H-1B only. ([Proclamation 11069 text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/09/23/2026-19554.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- The standard is "extraordinary ability ... by sustained national or international acclaim," meaning "one of the small percentage who have arisen to the very top of the field." A US employer or agent files Form I-129 with a written advisory opinion from a peer group or expert. ([USCIS O-1 page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement), last updated 03/03/2023, read 2026-10-20)
- The evidence is a major internationally recognized award, or **at least three** of these: national or international prizes; membership in associations that require outstanding achievement; published material about him in major media or trade press; judging the work of others; original contributions "of major significance"; scholarly articles; a "critical or essential capacity" role at distinguished organizations; a high salary. "Comparable evidence" is allowed if the criteria do not fit. ([8 CFR 214.2(o)(3)(iii), eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20)
- USCIS has STEM-specific guidance on these criteria in the Policy Manual, Vol. 2, Part M, Ch. 4. ([USCIS O-1 page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement), read 2026-10-20)
- **Feasibility for Devanshu: low right now.** A new grad seldom has three of these criteria. It is worth tracking as a two-to-three-year goal: for example, judging (hackathons, paper review), published work, a critical role at a well-regarded startup, and a high salary. This is my judgment, not a USCIS statement.

### 6.2 Day 1 CPT

- CPT must be "an integral part of an established curriculum." On Aug 12, 2026, SEVP told schools to "only authorize CPT if its absence would make the attainment of a degree an impossibility and if the work associated with CPT is required for all students pursuing that degree." It warned of HSI action and "potential withdrawal of the school's SEVP certification." ([SEVP Broadcast Message 2608-01](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/bcm260801.pdf), read 2026-10-20)
- A follow-up Q&A, Broadcast Message 2608-02 (Aug 24, 2026), is reported by secondary sources. I could not open the ICE file. ([BU ISSO](https://www.bu.edu/isso/2026/08/17/new-sevp-guidance-on-cpt-eligibility/), read 2026-10-20)
- The Sept 14, 2026 injunction against the duration of status rule did not affect these broadcast messages (secondary: [globalimmigrationblog](https://www.globalimmigrationblog.com/2026/09/federal-court-blocks-dhs-f-1-j-1-fixed-period-admission-rule-curricular-practical-training-restrictions-remain/), read 2026-10-20).
- **Risk:** enrolling in a second degree mainly to get Day 1 CPT work authorization now risks the school's authorization being found improper. That could hurt later H-1B, visa, or green card filings. Not a route to recommend.

### 6.3 Concurrent employment

- On OPT or STEM OPT he can hold more than one job, as long as each is directly related to his degree. On STEM OPT, each employer must meet all the rules (E-Verify, I-983, 20+ hours a week), and DHS expects more than two employers at once to be "very unusual, though not expressly prohibited." ([81 FR 13040 text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2016/03/11/2016-04828.txt), read 2026-10-20)
- After H-1B: a cap-exempt H-1B worker can add a concurrent cap-subject H-1B job without the lottery while the cap-exempt job continues (section 5.3). This is a real path: get into H-1B through a university or research nonprofit, then add or later move to private work. The catch is that if the cap-exempt job ends before he has been counted against the cap, he becomes cap-subject again. ([8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(F)(6)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/section-214.2), read 2026-10-20)

### 6.4 Routes that do not apply to an Indian citizen

- **H-1B1** is only for citizens of Chile and Singapore ([8 U.S.C. 1184(g)(8)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title8-section1184&num=0&edition=prelim), read 2026-10-20).
- **TN** is only for citizens of Canada and Mexico ([USCIS TN page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/tn-usmca-professionals), read 2026-10-20).
- **E-3** is only for Australian citizens ([USCIS E-3 page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/e-3-specialty-occupation-workers-from-australia), read 2026-10-20).

**Totals for an F-1 change of status today (no premium processing, no proclamation payment):**

- A company with 26 or more FTE: $215 + $730 online + $600 + $500 + $1,500 = **$3,545** (or $3,595 on paper).
- A cap-exempt university or 501(c)(3) research nonprofit: no registration, $460 + $0 + $500 + $0 = **$960**.
- **If the proposed $103,265 fee is finalized**, add $103,265 to every cap-subject petition, his included. Cap-exempt employers would not pay it (section 1.4).

- **Attorney fees (secondary sources only):** commonly quoted at $2,500 to $5,000 for an initial cap petition and $2,000 to $4,000 for a cap-exempt petition ([Beyond Border Global](https://www.beyondborderglobal.com/resources/h-1b-visa-lawyer-fees), read 2026-10-20).
- **Who pays:** DOL rules bar the employer from recouping through wage deductions "attorney fees and other costs connected to the performance of H-1B program functions which are required to be performed by the employer, e.g., preparation and filing of LCA and H-1B petition." - **New scrutiny:** EO 14431 directs agencies to weigh an employer's layoffs in the prior year when deciding LCAs, petitions, and visas (section 1.1).

## 8. What this means for targeting

This section uses only the facts above. Where I judge or estimate, I say so.

### 8.1 Employer traits, ranked by how much they matter

1. **E-Verify enrolled (a hard gate for any job that lasts past Jul 8, 2027).** Without it he cannot get STEM OPT, and his work authorization ends Jul 8, 2027 unless an H-1B petition is filed for him by then (cap-gap). He needs a signed I-983 from an E-Verify employer in his STEM filing window (about Apr 9 to Jul 8, 2027). Enrollment is free, and anyone can check it with the [E-Verify employer search](https://www.e-verify.gov/employers/enrolling-in-e-verify). Weight: highest. Treat "not enrolled and unwilling to enroll" as a stop for any role he wants to keep past mid-2027.
2. **Cap-exempt** (university, university-affiliated nonprofit, nonprofit or government research organization, or a role where at least half his time furthers such an organization's mission). No lottery, filing any month, no ACWIA fee, about $960 in government fees, and no exposure to the proposed $103,265 fee. Weight: very high. It also opens the concurrent cap-exempt path in 6.3. It needs a case-by-case check of 501(c)(3) status and research as a "fundamental activity."
3. **Will pay at or above level II for his SOC and metro.** For SOC 15-1252 in Boston (OFLC 2026-27), level I starts at $114,275, level II at $138,237, level III at $162,198 (from growth-signals.md). Level II roughly keeps the old random-lottery odds; level I roughly halves them (DHS: 15.29 vs 30.58 percent, against 29.59 random). Weight: high, but only for cap-subject employers and only in March of each lottery year.
4. **Direct employer, not a staffing or consulting firm that places workers at clients.** The 2016 STEM rule says "temp" agencies and labor-for-hire arrangements do not fit STEM OPT. The proclamations and EO 14431 single out IT staffing and outsourcing. Weight: high as a negative filter.
5. **Has filed H-1B or LCAs before.** This signals an employer that knows the process and has counsel. Its LCA history also shows what wage levels it tends to offer (8.2). Weight: medium. The data is DOL OFLC LCA disclosure files, latest FY2026 Q3 ([DOL OFLC performance data](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/foreign-labor/performance), read 2026-10-20).
6. **Size of 25 or fewer FTE.** ACWIA $750 instead of $1,500, asylum fee $300 instead of $600, I-129 $460 instead of $730 or $780. That is about $2,225 versus $3,545 in government fees. Weight: low. The saving is about $1,300, small next to salary, and next to the $103,265 proposal if it is finalized.
7. **No layoffs in the past year.** EO 14431 tells agencies to weigh them on LCAs, petitions and visas. Weight: low to medium. It is new, and how agencies apply it is not yet known.
8. **Not affected by the $100,000 payment.** Every employer hiring him is in the same position: change of status from F-1 inside the US is exempt. This trait does not separate employers. Use it to calm them, not to rank them.

### 8.2 How to weigh LCA history (level I vs level II)

- What counts in the lottery is the level his **own** offered wage reaches, not the employer's history. History is a predictor of what the employer will offer, so use the posted salary first (growth-signals.md method) and LCA history second.
- Suggested weights (my judgment, from the DHS odds): level II or higher history about 2x a level I-only history for cap-subject employers. Level III or IV history means the employer pays well above market and is a strong signal.
- **Multiple registrations drag him down.** If two employers register him in the same March, USCIS uses the **lowest** level among them ([90 FR 60864 text](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2025/12/29/2025-23853.txt), read 2026-10-20). A second employer registering him at level I would cut the odds a level II employer earned. Tell employers this before March.
- Several worksites also use the lowest level. A remote role tied to a cheaper metro may score a higher level for the same salary (for SOC 15-1252, Austin's level II starts at $119,642 versus Boston's $138,237). Not verified how each employer will file.
- DOL's proposed wage rule (2.5) would raise the level cutoffs if finalized. Re-score LCA history and postings against the new tables when that happens.

Lottery chances over the three Marches, **my estimates**, assuming each year looks like the base year and he is registered every year:

| Per-year odds source | Level I, one year | Level I, three years | Level II, one year | Level II, three years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DHS final rule estimate (larger pool) | 15.3% | 39% | 30.6% | 67% |
| FY2027-based estimate (211,600 pool, 95,000 to 120,141 selections) | 26% to 35% | 59% to 72% | 45% to 57% | 83% to 92% |

The three-year figure is 1 - (1 - p)^3. These ignore his extra master's-round chance, which helps a little. They also ignore changes such as the $103,265 fee (which DHS expects to shrink the pool) or the DOL wage rule. Without STEM OPT he gets one lottery (March 2027) instead of three. That is why E-Verify matters most.

### 8.4 A fact list he can give a nervous small employer

Each line is backed by a source above. Keep the dates.

1. "Hiring me now costs you nothing extra. I'm on OPT until July 8, 2027. You do a normal I-9, same as any hire." (4.1)
2. "To keep me after that, on STEM OPT for up to 24 more months, you need to be enrolled in E-Verify, which is free and online, and sign a training plan (Form I-983) with my school. There's no USCIS petition and no USCIS fee." (4.2, 7.2)
3. "STEM OPT works at startups and small companies, as long as I'm your direct employee, on payroll at least 20 hours a week, and paid like a US hire in the same role." (4.2)
4. "The $100,000 H-1B payment doesn't apply to me. USCIS says it does not apply to a change of status for someone already in the US, and I'm here on F-1. USCIS isn't collecting it right now anyway, because of a June 2026 court order." (1.2, 1.4)
5. "If you ever sponsor an H-1B, government fees for a company of 25 or fewer employees are about $2,225 today, plus a lawyer. One thing to know: DHS has proposed an extra $103,265 fee on every lottery-based H-1B. It's a proposal, not final, as of October 2026." (7.3, 1.4)
6. "There's no H-1B decision to make until March 2027. With STEM OPT I'm covered through mid-2029, which gives us three lottery tries." (8.3)
7. "Under the new lottery, a salary at wage level II or higher gets my registration twice the entries of level I." (2.2)
8. (Universities and research nonprofits only) "As a cap-exempt employer you can file an H-1B any time with no lottery, and the proposed extra fee would not apply to you." (5.3)

Do not tell employers that OPT is safe long term. The proposed OPT fee and the planned "Practical Training" rule (4.3) are real risks.
